How the grading works

What gets graded. For each of the last 30 full days, we take what each model predicted the day's high and low would be 1, 2 and 3 days beforehand, and whether it predicted rain.

What it's graded against. Every model is compared with the same reference: Open-Meteo's historical weather record for that spot, the standard best estimate of what actually happened. Grading each model against its own later runs would let it mark its own homework.

The grade. The letter is the average miss on the high and low one day ahead: A under 2°F (1.1°C), B under 3°F, C under 4°F, D under 5°F, F at 5°F or more. Rain is scored as the share of days the model correctly called wet (1 mm or more) or dry.

The Evolution Lab. A genetic algorithm evolves 48 blending recipes for 60 generations. Each recipe gives every model a share of trust and adds a correction to the high and low. Recipes are scored only on the first two-thirds of the month (practice). The last third is kept sealed as an exam, and the champion counts as trusted only if it beats the best single model there and the 90% range of its improvement stays above zero.

Limits. The reference is itself a model-based estimate for a grid square, not your backyard thermometer, and it is built partly from European (ECMWF) data, which may slightly favor the ECMWF model. Thirty days is one season's worth of weather, not a permanent verdict, so grades can change as the weather does.
